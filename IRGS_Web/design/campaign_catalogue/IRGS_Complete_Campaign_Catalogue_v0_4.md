# IRGS Launch Catalogue — Expanded Campaign Concepts

## TIER I — SHORT STORIES

**18 launch titles | Free at launch | Focused personal experiences**

### SS-01 — The Long Road Home

**Tagline:** Two survivors. Hostile territory. One uncertain road home.

**Setting:** Sengoku-period Japan, with the exact province, date, and military affiliations established through historical configuration.

**Genres:** Survival, Action, Adventure, Thriller, Historical

An ambush separates a Japanese retainer and his loyal servant from their military force. Stranded beyond friendly lines, they must survive unfamiliar territory, manage their dwindling supplies, and attempt to return home. Villages, patrols, travelers, temples, and rival forces may offer danger, opportunity, or assistance.

**Playable perspectives:** The retainer or the servant. The unselected character becomes an independently acting companion.

**Featured systems:** Character relationships, survival, injuries, travel, stealth, equipment, supplies, navigation, reputation, and NPC autonomy.

**Distinctive experience:** The same journey changes considerably depending on which member of the pair the player controls. Social status, training, and authority affect available options.

### SS-02 — Before the Battle

**Tagline:** Every battle begins long before the armies meet.

**Setting:** Player-configurable feudal conflict, with one or two historically appropriate nations or political powers on each side.

**Genres:** Historical, Military, Action, Slice of Life, Variable

Two opposing alliances prepare for battle. Join either side in any feasible occupation, from soldier, knight, commander, or archer to blacksmith, cook, merchant, messenger, or engineer. Experience recruitment, preparation, supply difficulties, military planning, ordinary camp life, battle, and aftermath.

**Playable perspectives:** Any eligible military or civilian role associated with the armies and their support networks.

**Featured systems:** Military organization, command, logistics, economy, occupations, equipment, morale, battle simulation, and casualty continuity.

**Distinctive experience:** The player's role determines how they experience the event, but the armies and wider battle continue operating independently.

### SS-03 — First Encounters

**Tagline:** Two worlds meet. Neither knows what comes next.

**Setting:** Historically supported North American region and encounter period, selected during scenario configuration.

**Genres:** Historical, Drama, Exploration, Diplomacy, Variable

Experience an early encounter between a First Nations community and European arrivals. Choose the societies, circumstances, and individual perspective. The encounter may involve trade, diplomacy, uncertainty, cultural exchange, competition, hostility, or attempts at cooperation.

**Playable perspectives:** Indigenous community members or European newcomers in historically feasible roles.

**Featured systems:** Culture, languages, translation, knowledge, diplomacy, institutions, trade, social relationships, and information transmission.

**Distinctive experience:** Neither side automatically understands the other's intentions. Both communities have their own interests, histories, political relationships, and decision-making.

**Development requirement:** Specific Indigenous nations, their perspectives, land relationships, and documented histories require dedicated research and appropriate consultation.

### SS-04 — The Hunted

**Tagline:** In the wilderness, the danger is already moving.

**Setting:** Configurable forested environment; contemporary realism is the proposed introductory setting.

**Genres:** Horror, Survival, Thriller, Action

A dangerous pursuit unfolds in an isolated region. Experience the situation as the person being hunted or as the killer. Surrounding terrain, weather, buildings, available equipment, other people, and incomplete information influence how the encounter develops.

**Playable perspectives:** The hunted individual or the killer.

**Featured systems:** Perception, stealth, navigation, evidence, fatigue, stress, injuries, autonomous opponents, and environmental conditions.

**Distinctive experience:** Neither perspective receives omniscient knowledge. The characters must act on what they can observe, infer, remember, or learn.

### SS-05 — The Northern Frontier

**Tagline:** An ordinary day on Rome's frontier becomes a fight for survival.

**Setting:** Hadrian's Wall, Roman Britannia, with a proposed late-second-century starting period.

**Genres:** Historical, Military, Action, Survival

Serve as a Roman garrison soldier on the empire's northern frontier. Carry out normal military duties, interact with fellow soldiers and civilians, and experience the disruption of an attack by northern forces. Your actual assignment, location, training, equipment, and available orders determine your involvement.

**Playable perspectives:** A Roman garrison soldier, with eligible ranks and duties determined during character setup.

**Featured systems:** Garrison routines, military discipline, fortifications, command, equipment, injuries, logistics, and coordinated combat.

**Distinctive experience:** The garrison is an operational institution, not merely the backdrop for a scripted fight.

### SS-06 — The Initiation

**Tagline:** Membership has a price. Whether you pay it is another matter.

**Setting:** Configurable urban criminal environment, with a contemporary fictional city as the introductory option.

**Genres:** Crime, Drama, Thriller, Social Simulation

A prospective gang member approaches an initiation ceremony that may alter their place within a criminal organization. Meet existing members, understand internal expectations, manage relationships, and respond to the initiation and its consequences.

**Playable perspectives:** A prospective or affiliated member, with selectable personal background, sponsorship, and existing relationships.

**Featured systems:** Reputation, organizational hierarchy, membership, loyalty, coercion, personal relationships, obligations, criminal consequences, and social autonomy.

**Distinctive experience:** The initiation is a living social event. Players may cooperate, refuse, negotiate, or pursue other feasible choices.

### SS-07 — The Price of Bread

**Tagline:** When food becomes scarce, every household feels the consequences.

**Setting:** Proposed ancient Mesopotamian community, around 1800 BCE.

**Genres:** Historical, Economic Drama, Slice of Life, Survival

A difficult harvest threatens the food supply of a functioning community. Farmers, millers, traders, laborers, officials, and families respond according to their own resources and circumstances. Prices, wages, storage, obligations, and trade may change as shortages develop.

**Playable perspectives:** Farmer, baker or food producer, merchant, worker, official, or ordinary household member.

**Featured systems:** Agricultural production, storage, markets, household budgets, labor, taxation, contracts, and scarcity.

**Distinctive experience:** The crisis is resolved through the actual movement and use of resources rather than an arbitrary economic event counter.

### SS-08 — The Broken Compass

**Tagline:** The ocean does not care where you intended to go.

**Setting:** Proposed late-eighteenth-century Pacific voyage.

**Genres:** Maritime, Survival, Adventure, Drama

A ship suffers a major disruption while traveling across open water. Damage, weather, supplies, navigation, and crew competence determine its changing circumstances. Characters must deal with the immediate emergency and the longer-term challenge of reaching safety.

**Playable perspectives:** Captain, navigator, sailor, carpenter, passenger, or another suitable role aboard the vessel.

**Featured systems:** Sailing, navigation, weather, structural damage, shipboard hierarchy, provisions, maintenance, and travel.

**Distinctive experience:** The ship is a functioning mobile environment with interdependent systems and finite resources.

### SS-09 — An Empty Throne

**Tagline:** A ruler has died. Authority remains, but certainty does not.

**Setting:** Proposed thirteenth- or fourteenth-century West African Sahelian polity, to be selected through historical research.

**Genres:** Politics, Historical Drama, Intrigue, Social Simulation

The death of a ruler creates uncertainty concerning succession, property, offices, and political loyalties. Members of the court and wider society respond according to their relationships, knowledge, legal traditions, and personal interests.

**Playable perspectives:** Eligible claimant, relative, official, court servant, merchant, military representative, or other connected individual.

**Featured systems:** Political legitimacy, succession, institutions, diplomacy, relationships, information, property, and authority.

**Distinctive experience:** The player need not seek the throne. Ordinary lives continue while political institutions negotiate the transition.

### SS-10 — The Last Crossing

**Tagline:** The river is rising, and the community must decide what to save.

**Setting:** Proposed Qing-era Chinese river settlement, around 1700.

**Genres:** Disaster, Survival, Historical Drama

Severe flooding threatens homes, fields, storage sites, and transport routes. Residents and authorities act according to available information, local procedures, equipment, and competing obligations.

**Playable perspectives:** Ferryman, farmer, laborer, merchant, resident, local official, or other feasible participant.

**Featured systems:** Water and environmental hazards, infrastructure, evacuation, transport, property, rescue, labor coordination, and institutional response.

**Distinctive experience:** The flood behaves as a developing environmental event. Different characters face different practical responsibilities and consequences.

### SS-11 — A Wedding in Spring

**Tagline:** Two lives come together, bringing two households with them.

**Setting:** Proposed Joseon Korea, around 1700.

**Genres:** Slice of Life, Romance, Family, Historical Drama

A marriage approaches, bringing together relatives, guests, household responsibilities, finances, traditions, and personal relationships. Preparations proceed alongside everyday work, changing expectations, and family obligations.

**Playable perspectives:** Either prospective spouse, a relative, guest, household worker, or another participant with an appropriate connection.

**Featured systems:** Households, family relationships, marriage institutions, ceremonies, inheritance, expenses, reputation, and daily routines.

**Distinctive experience:** A peaceful social event receives the same simulation depth ordinarily reserved for dangerous or military scenarios.

### SS-12 — Ashes on the Waterfront

**Tagline:** One fire can change an entire neighborhood.

**Setting:** Proposed East African Indian Ocean port, around 1600.

**Genres:** Disaster, Action, Historical Drama, Investigation

A fire spreads through a populated waterfront district. Buildings, cargo, weather, access routes, emergency resources, and the decisions of residents determine the consequences. The player may become involved in rescue, containment, evacuation, protecting livelihoods, or subsequent investigation.

**Playable perspectives:** Merchant, dockworker, sailor, watchman, property owner, resident, or local official.

**Featured systems:** Fire propagation, construction materials, inventory, rescue, injuries, property damage, commercial losses, and coordinated response.

**Distinctive experience:** The emergency affects a functioning local economy rather than an isolated encounter zone.

### SS-13 — Across the Pass

**Tagline:** Goods, people, and promises must cross the mountains together.

**Setting:** Proposed Central Asian trade route, around 1100.

**Genres:** Adventure, Commerce, Travel, Survival

A caravan must complete a difficult journey through mountainous terrain. Weather, pack animals, provisions, market conditions, local authorities, and relationships among travelers influence progress.

**Playable perspectives:** Trader, guide, guard, porter, animal handler, passenger, or another feasible caravan participant.

**Featured systems:** Logistics, transport, animal care, trade, weather, road conditions, contracts, and navigation.

**Distinctive experience:** Commerce and cooperation are as important as danger. Successful travel requires accounting for people, animals, cargo, and time.

### SS-14 — The Magistrate's Table

**Tagline:** Every dispute has a history. Every judgment has consequences.

**Setting:** Proposed Ottoman Anatolia, around 1600.

**Genres:** Legal Drama, Historical, Politics, Slice of Life

A local court hears disputes concerning property, contracts, responsibilities, personal conduct, or other matters within its jurisdiction. Evidence, legal procedure, institutional authority, witnesses, and social relationships influence the proceedings.

**Playable perspectives:** Magistrate or other eligible judicial officer, clerk, claimant, respondent, witness, legal representative, or observer.

**Featured systems:** Legal institutions, documentation, testimony, evidence, property, obligations, reputation, and enforcement.

**Distinctive experience:** A noncombat scenario tests whether IRGS can represent meaningful procedural and institutional decisions.

### SS-15 — The Last Train Through

**Tagline:** The journey has stopped. The danger has not.

**Setting:** Proposed early-twentieth-century Andean railway, around 1910.

**Genres:** Historical, Survival, Thriller, Industrial Drama

A railway journey is interrupted in a remote and physically demanding environment. Mechanical problems, weather, access routes, crew expertise, passenger needs, and available materials shape the response.

**Playable perspectives:** Engineer, conductor, maintenance worker, passenger, railway official, or other appropriate character.

**Featured systems:** Industrial equipment, mechanical failures, transport scheduling, repairs, environmental conditions, supplies, and group coordination.

**Distinctive experience:** Technical competence and routine procedures can avert disaster without requiring arbitrary failure rolls or forced escalation.

### SS-16 — Blackout Shift

**Tagline:** When essential systems fail, the people keeping them running matter most.

**Setting:** Proposed contemporary West African urban hospital.

**Genres:** Medical Drama, Modern, Disaster, Slice of Life

A hospital experiences a serious infrastructure disruption during an active shift. Clinical and facilities personnel must respond within their qualifications, available resources, institutional procedures, and obligations to patients.

**Playable perspectives:** Nurse, physician, technician, facilities worker, administrator, patient, or another feasible participant.

**Featured systems:** Organizations, occupations, healthcare resources, time-sensitive decisions, infrastructure, maintenance, supply chains, and institutional coordination.

**Distinctive experience:** The scenario tests skilled professional activity and complex resource dependencies outside warfare.

### SS-17 — Pressure Falling

**Tagline:** In orbit, every system matters.

**Setting:** Original science-fiction orbital facility, proposed twenty-third century.

**Genres:** Science Fiction, Survival, Thriller, Technical Drama

An orbital station suffers a serious technical failure affecting its ability to operate safely. Crew members, passengers, and automated systems respond according to their knowledge and capabilities. The situation may be stabilized, worsen, or develop unexpected but causally supported consequences.

**Playable perspectives:** Engineer, maintenance technician, medical worker, security officer, station administrator, or passenger.

**Featured systems:** Life support, engineering, technical diagnostics, environmental hazards, communications, equipment, and finite resources.

**Distinctive experience:** Physical dependencies and coordinated technical work are central to the scenario.

### SS-18 — The Missing Manuscript

**Tagline:** A missing book may reveal far more than its contents.

**Setting:** Proposed tenth-century Baghdad, around 950.

**Genres:** Mystery, Investigation, Historical Drama, Scholarship

A valuable manuscript disappears from a scholarly or private collection. Its loss creates questions involving ownership, access, institutional relationships, scholarly interests, and the credibility of witnesses.

**Playable perspectives:** Scholar, scribe, librarian, patron, investigator, attendant, or another connected individual.

**Featured systems:** Knowledge, literacy, records, evidence, memory, investigation, social networks, property, and information security.

**Distinctive experience:** The mystery must be solvable through consistent records and actor knowledge, without changing established evidence to fit the player's suspicions.

### MC-01 — Century of Conflict: The Hundred-Years' War

**Tagline:** A century of conflict. Thousands of lives. History is not yet written.

**Setting:** Medieval Western Europe, beginning at supported historical entry points between 1337 and 1453, with persistent continuation thereafter.

**Genres:** Historical, Military, Political, Economic, Life Simulation, Adventure

Inhabit the world of the Hundred Years' War through the lives of nobles, commoners, merchants, soldiers, independent contractors, officials, scholars, households, and institutions. Historical events establish starting conditions, but the world's development responds to actual decisions and circumstances. Kingdoms and armies act beyond the player's immediate experience.

**Entry perspectives:** Independently chosen backgrounds, occupations, institutions, affiliations, and eligible character or organizational perspectives. No mandatory faction service.

**Major development scope:** Four interconnected Full Campaign-equivalent domains representing appropriate political, military, economic, and civilian theaters.

**Featured IRGS systems:** Diplomacy, taxation, warfare, logistics, regional administration, historical information, dynastic succession, social mobility, production, and generational history.

**Campaign distinction:** A historically grounded world with supported historical entry points and extensive scope for alternate outcomes. Its central historical focus is the Hundred Years' War, making Major Campaign the adopted classification.

### MC-02 — The Bronze Age Powers

**Tagline:** Empires trade, negotiate, fight, and depend upon one another.

**Setting:** Eastern Mediterranean and Ancient Near East, proposed c. 1400–1150 BCE.

**Genres:** Historical, Civilization, Political, Military, Economic Simulation

Explore the interconnected societies of ancient Egypt, Anatolia, the Levant, and Mesopotamia during a period of powerful kingdoms, regional trade, diplomacy, and institutional change. Royal courts, local communities, temples, workshops, caravans, military organizations, and agricultural production interact across political borders.

**Entry perspectives:** Farmer, merchant, craftworker, soldier, scribe, diplomatic representative, administrator, religious official, household member, or another historically grounded participant.

**Major development scope:** Egypt and the Nile; Anatolia; Levantine states and cities; Mesopotamian states and communities.

**Featured IRGS systems:** Interstate diplomacy, agriculture, palace and temple economies, taxation, military organization, resource dependencies, production, legal authority, and regional trade.

**Campaign distinction:** Demonstrates complex multistate simulation with ancient technologies and institutions. Historical crises may develop, but the simulation does not force a predetermined civilizational collapse.

## TIER V — GRAND CAMPAIGN

**1 launch title | Civilization-scale living world**

### GC-01 — The World of Kira

**Tagline:** Anyone. Anywhere. Anyhow.

**Setting:** The established fictional World of Kira, with supported historical periods and regions developed from its existing lore.

**Genres:** Open-World Fantasy, Life Simulation, Political, Military, Economic, Adventure, Civilization Simulation

The World of Kira is a persistent, inhabited world in which individuals, households, settlements, organizations, kingdoms, and civilizations pursue their own goals and respond to changing circumstances. Players choose who they are, where they begin, and how they wish to live.

Its established lore includes the Akarian Empire, Sadion, Everwood, Kyrinus, Estru, and the wider geographic and cultural foundations associated with Larius and TalmahKah. These foundations will be expanded while preserving existing canon and documenting deliberate changes.

A player may seek employment, build a household, establish commercial enterprises, travel, study, serve an institution, pursue political influence, participate in military affairs, or choose a quieter life.

**Entry perspectives:** Any setting-feasible individual or eligible institutional perspective, with no predetermined protagonist, faction allegiance, or required career.

**Grand development scope:** Multiple interdependent civilizations and regions operating simultaneously across extensive geographic and chronological scales.

**Featured IRGS systems:** The integrated Core simulation, autonomous civilizations, households, relationships, production, commerce, institutions, warfare, diplomacy, culture, technology, knowledge, ecology, and persistent historical continuity.

**Campaign distinction:** Kira is not a story about one war, kingdom, dynasty, or individual. It is a living world in which the player's story emerges through participation.

**Development dependency:** Existing Kira source material remains authoritative where adopted. Unresolved geography, cultures, magic, chronology, and initial state require reconciliation and expansion before release.



The launch selection provides a progressive increase in systemic scope: personal incidents, communities, interconnected regions, multistate historical environments, and finally a complete living world.

None of these tiers should force players into combat, leadership, or institutional service simply because the simulation is capable of representing those activities.

## Part V — Content development requirements

To convert these concepts into formal development projects, each needs the following underlying work.

| Phase                                 | Deliverable                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1. Concept approval                   | Accepted title, premise, scope, classification, and featured components        |
| 2. Historical or fictional foundation | Geography, history, cultures, institutions, technology, environment            |
| 3. Player entry design                | Perspectives, origins, professions, starting resources, relationships          |
| 4. Simulation integration             | Core interfaces, Genre Engine dependencies, setting rules                      |
| 5. Living-world population            | Autonomous individuals, households, organizations, and resources               |
| 6. Scenario implementation            | Initial circumstances, opportunities, ongoing processes, possible outcomes     |
| 7. Verification                       | Agency, continuity, logistics, institutional operation, historical consistency |
| 8. Publication                        | Approved content, playable initial state, public catalogue listing             |

For higher tiers, those requirements extend across multiple interacting communities, regions, or civilizations. A Grand Campaign is not simply a Full Campaign with a larger map.

Historical entries also need research and provenance review before their proposed dates, political institutions, or cultural details are promoted to established setting facts.

## Portfolio assessment

The combined catalogue gives IRGS substantial variety:

- 18 Short Stories demonstrate concentrated situations and individual-level mechanics.
- 9 Curated Campaign concepts demonstrate social and community simulation.
- 6 Full Campaign concepts demonstrate interacting regional systems.
- 6 Major Campaign concepts demonstrate multiregional political, economic, and social systems.
- 4 Grand Campaign concepts demonstrate civilization-scale persistence.
- Rise of \_\_\_\_\_\_ demonstrates customizable household and settlement simulation as its own product category.

The immediate 31-title launch slate remains unchanged, and the additional twelve campaigns form a future development pipeline.

The strongest feature of this structure is that every campaign has two purposes: provide a distinct player experience and exercise a meaningful combination of IRGS systems. As the catalogue expands, the system can gain broader historical and fictional coverage without developing redundant engines.

The next formal step is to turn the expanded descriptions into individual campaign concept records, with their approval states, content dependencies, launch positioning, and Core integration requirements. That will allow them to be displayed in the app while their deeper foundations are developed separately.
